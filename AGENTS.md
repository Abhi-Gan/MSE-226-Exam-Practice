MS&E 226: Fundamentals of Data Science — midterm and final

These criteria describe the goals used to design and review exam questions.
The relevant exam review, current course materials, and syllabus define the
scope and exam rules.

Stay within the announced scope. All reasoning needed to answer a
   question should come from the examinable lectures, required homework, and
   course prerequisites. A new application may ask students to transfer their
   understanding to an unfamiliar setting, without requiring outside knowledge
   of that application.

Have one clearly correct complete response. The design criterion is:
   **“One obviously correct answer and three clearly incorrect but plausible
   distractors. Identifying the correct answer should require the intended
   understanding.”** The answer should be clear after reasoning; it need not
   be easy to discover. For questions with “both,” “neither,” or “all of the
   above,” the complete response must be unambiguous.

Test understanding through meaningful alternatives. Incorrect choices
   should reflect recognizable misconceptions or reasoning errors. Questions
   should avoid artificial tricks, implausible alternatives, and wording that
   gives away the answer.

Include focused and integrative reasoning. A focused question tests one
   coherent distinction. An integrative question requires connecting concepts
   to determine the answer. Listing several unrelated facts does not by itself
   make a question integrative.

State the setting precisely and concisely. Questions should distinguish
   population quantities from sample estimates, fitted models from learning
   algorithms, and fixed quantities from random ones when these affect the
   answer. Necessary assumptions and approximations should be clear. Difficulty
   should come from the statistical reasoning rather than avoidable ambiguity.

Keep calculation purposeful. Use simple numbers and arithmetic feasible
   without a calculator. Selecting an appropriate formula, interpreting a
   quantity, or comparing methods can be as important as calculating a number.
   Context and notation should support the reasoning without unnecessary detail.

Balance the exam as a whole. Include foundational conceptual checks,
   applications, and more demanding synthesis. Seek variety in topics and
   reasoning, with a workload appropriate to the available time. Questions may
   share a setup, but each answer should be recoverable without knowing another
   question's answer.

Review both correctness and design. Work through the intended solution
   and every competing response. Check scope, assumptions, clarity, and the
   connection required by integrative questions. Review the complete exam for
   coverage, repeated reasoning, unintended clues, and workload.
